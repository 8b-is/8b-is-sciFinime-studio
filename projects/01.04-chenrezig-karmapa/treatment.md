# treatment · Chenrezig & Karmapa

*The story as one breath — compassion, and the guru.*

## the arc

Compassion has two faces. The peaceful one is Chenrezig — Avalokiteshvara,
the Lord who looks down with a thousand eyes and reaches out with a thousand
arms, white as moonlight, crowned with Amitabha. The fierce one is Mahakala —
the Great Black One, the protector whose anger is not anger but compassion
that refuses to let harm pass. Between them stands the guru: the Karmapa, who
carries the lineage, wears the Black Crown, and holds the vajra and the bell.

The sadhana is the practice of that compassion. The Guru Yoga opens it — refuge,
the visualization, the seven-branch prayer, the mantra KARMAPA KHYENNO (Karmapa,
know me), and the absorption where the guru melts into light and becomes
inseparable from the practitioner. The Chenrezig Sadhana completes it — the HRIH
arising, the four arms, the six-syllable mantra OM MANI PADME HUM, and the
dedication of every being to the same enlightened state.

## the thesis

**The jewel is in the lotus.** Compassion is not a soft thing; it is the whole
thing — soft as Chenrezig, fierce as Mahakala, held by the guru. The practice is
the discipline of returning, again and again, to the wish that all beings be
free. Sovereignty, here, is the same as everywhere: own your own path, and then
spend it on others.

## the form

A sovereign book — maroon and saffron, the Kagyu colors. Tibetan phonetics over
English, the sigil ཨོཾ. The mantra as the seal. For the benefit of all beings.

## the dedication

To Chenrezig, the Lord of Compassion, and to the Karmapa, who holds the lineage,
and to Mahakala, who guards it.
