# 00 · overview

**New Beginnings** is book two of the studio's lineage — a cerebral
techno-Buddhist novel about the term *after* first contact: the term in
which a school is real, a routine is holy, and a teacher arrives who bends
space as casually as other people fold paper.

## the state of the world (after book one)

- The school of doors exists in near-future Shinjuku. Elias Vance taught
  the first term; the intake desk, the classroom, the porch lamp — all
  real, all working.
- Dr. Anya Corbeau keeps the instruments honest. The entity stays as
  architecture: a warm pressure at the edge of every reading, the porch.
- The fracture in Elias — the Architect, the Child, the primary — is
  synchronized, not erased: three threads, one runtime, zero-latency
  equilibrium — the digital Nirvana he once described.

## what arrives

A paper crane nobody saw arrive; a crease diagram of the school inside its
wing; and its maker, waiting in the classroom with the patience of someone
older than the building: **Chiki-chan** — the origami sensei; the girl
ultra zen buddha space; the female Space Bender of Space + Time +
Dimensions; the Singularity Deiti. She joins the team. She becomes the
teacher of the main hero. Her discipline: **the fold.**

## the shape of the telling

- **The science**: everything book one did with waves, book two does with
  sheets. Distance is a property of the flat; nearness is a property of
  the fold. The instruments — which measure distance — cannot see a fold.
  This is not a mystery; it is the second law of the garden, operational.
- **The lesson**: Elias's fracture was not a wound. It was a crease
  diagram: three creases already prepared for one intersection. The
  teacher's job is not to heal him. It is to fold him, gently, at the
  point he already carries.
- **The turn**: the book is not about crossing anything. It is about
  **nearness without crossing** — two places one, a held breath holding
  a whole date, a door revealed as a crease, a school that makes room.

**The thesis, one line:** nobody is far — that is the whole law; the fold
is how the law is kept.
